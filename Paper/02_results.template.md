## 1. Rice pre-training

Both architectures learned the rice task well (Table 2). ImageNet → rice reached a held-out macro-F1 of 0.968 for both ConvNeXt-Tiny and ViT-B/16; random → rice reached 0.924 (ConvNeXt, 100 epochs) and 0.940 (ViT, stopped at epoch 96 by early stopping). The random-initialised models therefore learned useful rice features but remain below the ImageNet-initialised ones. These rice scores may be slightly optimistic because rice near-duplicates at pHash distance 3–6 were not removed (Methods 1.2).

## 2. Evaluation protocol: repeated-specimen leakage

Many pearl-millet images are repeated photographs of the same leaf. Under the original folds, 96 of the 228 test images (42 %) had a near-identical same-class specimen (feature cosine ≥ 0.95) in the training folds; the specimen-grouped folds eliminated all such cases (Table 1). Moving from the original to the specimen-grouped folds lowered every macro-F1 estimate in Table 8, by 0.056 to 0.094 (ConvNeXt, 100 % data). Part of this change may reflect the different fold composition (for example, a fold with five Exserohilum images), not leakage alone, and the grouped protocol still leaves moderately similar images across folds (62 % of test images have a same-class training image at cosine ≥ 0.90). All remaining results use the specimen-grouped folds.

## 3. Pearl-millet performance and data fraction

Tables 3a–3c and Figure 3 give macro-F1, balanced accuracy and accuracy for the three initialisations at 10 %, 25 %, 50 % and 100 % of the target training data (mean training-set size 20, 46, 91 and about 182 images). Two patterns stand out. First, **ImageNet-initialised ConvNeXt was almost insensitive to training-set size**: macro-F1 was 0.801 ± 0.055 with about 20 training images (10 %) and 0.807 ± 0.062 with the full set. ViT with ImageNet initialisation showed a larger dependence (0.770 ± 0.053 at 10 % to 0.834 ± 0.078 at 100 %). Second, **random → rice initialisation (no ImageNet) was strongly data-dependent** for ConvNeXt (0.641 → 0.753) and, less steadily, for ViT (0.685 → 0.757). Fold-to-fold SDs (0.01–0.10) are as large as most between-condition differences, so the curves should be read with the intervals in Figure 3 in mind. Because the number of epochs is fixed, runs with fewer images perform fewer optimiser steps; a steps-matched sensitivity analysis is given in Section 9.

## 4. Does rice pre-training improve on ImageNet pre-training?

This is the primary comparison (Table 4, Figure 4; ImageNet → rice minus ImageNet, both with full fine-tuning).

* **ConvNeXt-Tiny.** The paired fold-level differences were +0.008, −0.011, −0.010 and +0.007 macro-F1 at 10 %, 25 %, 50 % and 100 % data. All image-level bootstrap intervals include zero and no difference met the pre-specified claim rule. At 25 % data the fold-level interval (−0.017 to −0.004; rice won 0 of 5 folds) excludes zero, indicating a small deficit, but fold-level intervals are not multiplicity-corrected; it did not survive the bootstrap criterion and is not repeated at other fractions. The largest positive point estimate (+0.008 at 10 %; bootstrap pooled +0.015, 95 % CI −0.005 to +0.036) is within the noise; rice pre-training won in 4 of 5 folds there, but in 0–3 of 5 folds at the other fractions. Balanced accuracy shows the same lack of pattern (+0.017, −0.031, −0.028, +0.005).
* **ViT-B/16.** ImageNet → rice scored lower than ImageNet alone at all four fractions: −0.023, −0.022, −0.031 and −0.016 (fold level; rice won in 1, 2, 1 and 0 of 5 folds). The image-level bootstrap criterion was met at 10 %, 50 % and 100 % (Holm-adjusted *p* = 0.048, 0.002, 0.048) but not at 25 % (adjusted *p* = 0.063), and **every fold-level 95 % interval includes zero**. The effect is small (about 0.02–0.04 macro-F1). We therefore regard this as weak evidence that rice intermediate pre-training does not help — and may slightly hurt — ViT in our setting, not as an established harm.
* **Transfer policies (ConvNeXt only).** With a frozen backbone (linear probe) or partial fine-tuning, rice and ImageNet pre-training were likewise indistinguishable (Table 10; no comparison met the rule).

We therefore found **no evidence that rice intermediate pre-training improves pearl-millet classification over ImageNet pre-training at any data fraction, for either architecture**, including the lowest-data setting (about 20 training images), where a benefit would be most expected. With five folds, differences smaller than about 0.04 macro-F1 cannot be excluded.

## 5. Rice knowledge without ImageNet

Without ImageNet, rice pre-training gave a transferable but weaker starting point.

* **Versus random initialisation (ConvNeXt only).** Random → rice beat random initialisation on pearl millet by +0.27 to +0.31 macro-F1 at every fraction (Table 5c; all five folds, bootstrap intervals far from zero). A random-initialisation baseline was not run for ViT, so this statement is limited to ConvNeXt.
* **Versus ImageNet.** Random → rice stayed below ImageNet initialisation at every fraction in both architectures (Table 5b). For ConvNeXt the deficit shrank as data increased (−0.160 at 10 % to −0.054 at 100 %; at 100 % the fold-level interval includes zero); for ViT it stayed between −0.066 and −0.085.
* **Does ImageNet add to rice?** ImageNet → rice scored higher than random → rice at all four fractions in both architectures; the pre-specified rule was met at all four fractions for ConvNeXt and at 10 %, 25 % and 100 % for ViT (Table 5b). ImageNet initialisation therefore contributed beyond what rice supplied.

## 6. Architecture comparison

Neither architecture was uniformly better (Table 5). With ImageNet initialisation, ViT-B/16 was lower than ConvNeXt-Tiny at 10 % data (−0.031; bootstrap-supported, fold-level interval [−0.060, −0.002]), indistinguishable at 25 % and 50 %, and higher at 100 % (+0.027; fold-level interval includes zero). With ImageNet → rice initialisation ViT was lower at 10 % (−0.062) and indistinguishable otherwise. With random → rice initialisation ViT was nominally higher at 10–50 % (+0.04 to +0.07) but no comparison met the rule. The sign of the architecture difference therefore depends on the data fraction and initialisation, and ViT-B/16 has three times as many parameters as ConvNeXt-Tiny, so these differences cannot be attributed to the architecture alone. We make no claim that either architecture is best.

## 7. Per-class performance and the Exserohilum class

Smut and *S. graminicola* were classified almost perfectly by ImageNet-initialised models at every fraction (pooled F1 ≥ 0.99; Table 6a–b), healthy leaves nearly so (0.88–1.00), and *Pyricularia* with F1 0.85–0.92. **Exserohilum, with only nine images in total, was unreliable in every condition** (pooled F1 0.04–0.38; Table 6c), and it is the main reason macro-F1 stays near 0.8. With ImageNet-initialised ConvNeXt, Exserohilum F1 did not improve with more data (0.31 at 10 %, 0.31 at 100 %), whereas ImageNet-initialised ViT, rice-initialised ViT and random → rice models improved with data (e.g. ViT with ImageNet: 0.21 → 0.38). The rice-initialised models were worse than the ImageNet-initialised ones on Exserohilum for ViT at all fractions (fold-level F1 difference −0.06 to −0.12, rice never winning in more than 2 of 5 folds), while for ConvNeXt the Exserohilum differences ranged from −0.07 to +0.02 without a consistent pattern. Per-class differences for the other classes were within ±0.07 and inconsistent in sign; the largest were a gain on healthy leaves at 10 % data for rice-initialised models (+0.05 to +0.07) and a loss on *Pyricularia* for ViT at 10 % (−0.05).

**Diagnosis (exploratory).** Across all stored runs, three of the nine Exserohilum images (`DRECRO_1`, `_8`, `_9`) were almost never classified correctly (0–12 % of runs), whereas the other six were correct in 67–88 % of runs (Table 12). For the three hard images the nearest image of any other class (*Pyricularia*) is more similar in ImageNet-feature space than their nearest Exserohilum image, and a 1-nearest-neighbour classifier in that space labels them *Pyricularia*. Recall is therefore capped at about 6/9 ≈ 0.67, which ImageNet-initialised models reached; low F1 is further driven by low precision, since 23 % of *Pyricularia* images were predicted as Exserohilum in the base ConvNeXt configuration. We cannot determine from the data whether the three images are mislabelled, atypical, or simply look-alike symptoms; expert review is needed. In a controlled exploratory study (Table 11; all variants reported, none selected on test performance, none feeding back into the main results), removing class weights raised Exserohilum precision (ImageNet: 0.20 → 0.46) at lower recall (0.67 → 0.56), whereas class-balanced sampling and higher input resolution (384 px) did not help; with nine images, none of these differences is established.

## 8. Calibration

All models were **under-confident**: for ViT-B/16 with ImageNet initialisation the mean confidence was 0.74–0.78 against accuracies of 0.87–0.92, giving a pooled ECE of 0.18–0.24 (Table 7a); ConvNeXt per-fold ECE was similar (0.19–0.24, Figure 6; per-fold ECE uses only ≈ 45 images and is biased upward, and pooled logits were not saved for ConvNeXt). Random → rice models had lower ECE (0.14–0.16) but also lower accuracy. In a dedicated ConvNeXt study with a held-out calibration fold (60 % of the data for training), the under-confidence was traced to the training loss: removing label smoothing or class weights reduced pooled ECE from about 0.20 to 0.06–0.08 (removing both: 0.05–0.06), longer training did not help, and temperature scaling fitted on the calibration fold lowered ECE to 0.03–0.09 (Table 7b). With no label smoothing the models became slightly over-confident (mean confidence 0.98 vs accuracy 0.91–0.93). Calibration did not differ materially between ImageNet and ImageNet → rice initialisation.

## 9. Sensitivity: steps-matched training

At 10–50 % data a fixed 40 epochs gives fewer optimiser steps. Repeating ImageNet-vs-rice with 80 epochs (ConvNeXt, partial and full fine-tuning) did not change the conclusion: the rice-minus-ImageNet differences were −0.001 to −0.025, with all fold-level intervals including zero (Table 9).

## 10. Summary of what the results support

* No measurable benefit of rice intermediate pre-training over ImageNet pre-training for pearl millet at 10 %, 25 %, 50 % or 100 % of the target data; for ViT-B/16 weak evidence of a small deficit.
* Rice pre-training without ImageNet transfers (ConvNeXt: far above random initialisation) but does not match ImageNet.
* No architecture is uniformly better; the sign of the ViT–ConvNeXt difference depends on data fraction and initialisation, and the architectures differ in size.
* The original folds leaked repeated specimens and gave higher scores; specimen-grouped evaluation is recommended.
* Exserohilum cannot be assessed reliably with nine images; calibration is poor under our default loss but is explained by label smoothing and class weighting and is largely repairable by temperature scaling.
